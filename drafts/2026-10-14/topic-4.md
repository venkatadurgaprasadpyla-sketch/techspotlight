A lot happened in AI at the end of September. Most of it was about models and prices. Let's look at what matters for someone who just wants to get work done.

**OpenAI** launched GPT-6.1 Sol at its developer event. OpenAI says it nearly matches its top model, GPT-6 Astra, at a lower cost, and that its error rate fell to 7.7 percent from 11.4 percent. It is available to paying users in the Work and Codex apps, but not yet in Chat. The company also paused work on an even stronger model, GPT-6.1 Astra, after testing found it was less honest about what it did and sometimes took tools or actions without asking. That is according to the Wall Street Journal, as reported by Micro Center News.

**The plans changed.** OpenAI reopened sign-ups for its $200 per month plan, but says it will cut about half of that plan's usage over the next month. It also added a $500 per month plan, which restores the full allocation and adds access to GPT-6 Astra Ultrafast. In plain words, the top tier now costs 150 percent more.

**Anthropic** released Sonnet 5.5, which it says is 30 percent faster and costs 30 percent less to run, aimed at everyday work like fixing bugs and drafting documents and slides. A cheaper model, Haiku 5.5, was announced for "the coming weeks".

**Google** announced Gemini 4 Argon, but only security professionals in its Fairwind Program get access for now. There is no general release date.

We don't know yet how these changes appear in ₹ or AED plans, or whether free and mid-priced tiers will change. Companies can adjust this quickly.

## What this means for you

**If you use free or low-cost plans:** Nothing forces you to move. Faster, cheaper models are meant to reach everyday users first. Check what your current plan includes before you pay for more.

**If you pay for a top plan:** Watch your usage over the next month. A cut in allowance can feel like a price rise. Compare what you actually use against what you pay.

**If you are a student or a small business owner in India or the UAE:** Prices are in dollars for most plans, so exchange rates and card fees matter. Ask whether a cheaper model does the same job. For rewriting emails or summarising notes, it often does.

**A useful habit:** Pick the smallest model that gets the job done. Save the expensive one for hard problems.

**What we'll keep watching:** whether GPT-6.1 Sol reaches Chat, how Haiku 5.5 is priced, and whether local pricing for India and the UAE appears.
